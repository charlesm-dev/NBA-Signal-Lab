## 4. What the two batches support — and what they do not

**Supported as a historical directional interpretation (not a statistically representative market survey):** both sets heavily emphasized reliability, APIs, cloud, SQL, distributed-system reasoning, and tests. The second set contained more explicitly data-pipeline, observability and AI-oriented roles, but this may reflect the researcher’s sampling choices. A lower container mention rate in batch two does **not** prove container skills lost market value.

**Not supported:** exact market prevalence, trends over time, complete employer-source traceability, exact Django/FastAPI frequencies, a claim that every posting remains active, or a fresh verified 48-row analysis. No evidence justifies treating historical counts as audited facts.

### Implications for NBA Signal Lab

1. **Direct and strong:** Python, Django/DRF, FastAPI, REST, PostgreSQL/SQL, provider adapters, historical ingestion, deterministic analytics, tests, security, failure handling, observability, AWS and CI/CD.
2. **Earn through measured needs:** Redis caching, Celery/background jobs, Kafka/event fan-out, WebSockets, object storage, horizontal scaling and IaC.
3. **Transferable but not exact:** Java/Spring, Go, TypeScript/Node, C#/.NET, Azure/GCP, gRPC, GraphQL and Kubernetes; a Python portfolio project does not count as hands-on professional experience in those exact ecosystems.
4. **Not project-equivalent:** production on-call history, large-scale organizational collaboration, regulated-industry work experience, or operating a real employer’s high-traffic systems.
